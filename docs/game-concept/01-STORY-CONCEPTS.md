# TRAINING MODE: THE GAME — Story Research & 20 Story Concepts

> **Status:** Brainstorm menu — v0.1 · Companion to `00-MASTER-CONCEPT.md` (§9)
> Deep-research pass on what story hooks land with the target audience
> (gym-goers × anime fans × retro-game enthusiasts), followed by 20 short
> story concepts to pick from, merge, or elaborate.

---

## Part 1 — What the Research Says (verified findings)

- **Recreate the *memory* of 90s arcade stories, not the reality.** The
  Shredder's Revenge team (Tribute Games/Dotemu) said outright that the
  originals "feel old" when replayed — they rebuilt the *idealized memory*
  with the friction sanded off: accessible by default, punishing difficulty
  only as an opt-in Arcade mode. Keep Training Mode's story arcade-flavored
  and frictionless. *(Sources: Inverse, Game Developer deep dive — dev
  interviews, verified 3-0.)*
- **Simple story, dense in-jokes.** Retro revivals win their niche by
  packing recognizable deep-cut references into stages, bosses, music, and
  dialogue — not by deepening lore. Training Mode's plot should fit on one
  screen while the *stages* carry the gym-culture and anime fan service.
  *(Inverse dev interview, verified 3-0.)*
- **The gym-bro × anime demographic is real and mainstream-sized.** Major
  fitness influencers (Jesse James West, Lean Beef Patty, Chris Heria)
  recreate anime workouts and the content gets syndicated by Men's
  Health/AOL/Yahoo. This is a recognized commercial niche, not a fringe
  overlap. *(Verified 3-0; medium confidence — syndicated sources.)*
- **The shared anime canon is a specific shonen fight pool:** Dragon Ball Z,
  One Punch Man, Baki, One Piece, Demon Slayer. Its strongest engagement
  pattern is **concrete, attemptable training challenges** — Saitama's
  100 pushups/100 situps/100 squats/10km is a real-world benchmark this
  audience literally attempts (and famously fails on the run). Story beats
  built on named, trainable regimens map directly onto what they already do.
  *(Verified; medium confidence.)*
- **Anime fight choreography itself is treated as replicable content** —
  not just training montages. Bosses whose movesets visibly quote
  recognizable fight styles will land. *(Verified 3-0; medium confidence.)*
- **Fit for the seed concept:** the dojo-takeover redemption arc delivers a
  classic level-by-level boss ladder (arcade-simple) while the heel-turn
  adds just enough twist to feel fresh without becoming lore-heavy.

**Research caveats:** the gym×anime findings trace largely to one 2022
syndicated video cluster; the revival-design findings center on Shredder's
Revenge. No claims survived verification on beat-'em-up narrative structure
per se — the concepts below rest on the verified audience findings plus
genre craft. Open question worth watching: whether newer properties (Solo
Leveling, Kengan Ashura) have joined the canon by 2026.

## Part 2 — The 20 Story Concepts

### A. Seed variations — the dojo-takeover redemption arc (§9 seed)

1. **HOSTILE TAKEOVER** *(seed, straight version)* — Two enforcers from the
   city's biggest mega-gym are sent to crush every rival dojo and studio,
   one per stage. Each fallen master reluctantly teaches them a technique;
   halfway through, the trainees realize they're erasing the city's fighting
   soul. **Hook:** the final boss is your own CEO — and the credits roll on
   the founding of Training Mode, the gym where every discipline lives.

2. **FRANCHISE WARS** *(seed, cyberpunk skin)* — In neon Neo-Muscle City,
   MegaGym Corp is buying every gym it can't beat and sending its two star
   trainees to "close" the holdouts. Stage by stage — boxing basement,
   powerlifting warehouse, rooftop yoga temple — the corp's true plan (one
   workout, one brand, forever) is revealed. **Hook:** you built the
   monopoly; now smash it, absorbing every discipline you once destroyed.

3. **THE BELT COLLECTORS** *(seed, trophy structure)* — The protagonists are
   ordered to collect the master's belt from every dojo in the city as proof
   of conquest. When the final belt is taken, the belts fit together into an
   ancient symbol showing all disciplines were once one school. **Hook:** a
   literal collect-the-bosses arcade structure that pays off the unification
   theme in one pixel-art reveal.

4. **SPLIT CITY** *(seed, gang-war skin)* — The city is split down the
   middle — Iron Side (gyms, machines, mirrors) vs. Fist Side (dojos, mats,
   masters) — and the two protagonists are raised as soldiers of Iron Side.
   Sent across the line to conquer, they discover both sides are being
   played against each other by one promoter profiting off the war.
   **Hook:** Streets of Rage turf war where the twist is uniting the map,
   not clearing it.

5. **TWO ENFORCERS** *(seed, dual-protagonist version)* — The male trainee
   is sent by a fitness mega-chain to take the martial-arts dojos; the
   female trainee is sent by a martial-arts syndicate to take the gyms.
   They fight each other as mid-game rival bosses — then compare orders.
   **Hook:** co-op unlocks at the story midpoint when the rivals team up to
   take down both bosses.

6. **REPO REPS** *(seed, blue-collar version)* — Working off a debt to a
   loan-shark gym owner, the duo repossesses equipment from struggling
   dojos — every stage is a gym defending its last barbell. The masters
   don't fight for pride; they fight for their students. **Hook:** the
   heel-turn lands when a kid asks for his dojo back, and the final stage is
   returning everything you took, one boss at a time.

### B. Shonen structures — tournament, training, rival arcs

7. **KING OF THE GYM** *(tournament arc)* — A once-a-decade city tournament
   pits every discipline's master against each other, and two unknown
   trainees enter as a team. Each victory grants their opponent's signature
   technique. **Hook:** pure shonen tournament ladder — brackets on screen
   between stages, rival re-matches, and a mystery final entrant.

8. **THEY TOOK OUR GYM** — The beloved neighborhood gym is bulldozed
   overnight by a development corp, and its two last members punch through
   construction sites, corporate lobbies, and a penthouse to reach the CEO.
   **Hook:** River City Ransom-simple revenge — one sentence of setup, a
   whole city of catharsis.

9. **THE MASTER IS MISSING** — The protagonists' old trainer vanishes, and
   the only clue is a rival mega-dojo's logo on the gym door. Fight through
   eight franchise locations, each run by a stolen master forced to teach
   the enemy's system. **Hook:** the classic kidnapped-mentor arcade plot,
   with each rescued master joining your home gym's roster.

10. **100 DAYS** *(One Punch Man homage)* — An ancient regimen — 100
    pushups, 100 situps, 100 squats, 10km, every day — is said to make a
    fighter unbeatable, and everyone who tried it quit. Two trainees attempt
    it together while the city's champions line up to stop them finishing.
    **Hook:** directly nods to the Saitama benchmark this audience already
    attempts in real life; each stage is one "day" of the regimen.

11. **MIRROR MATCH** *(rival arc)* — Your best training partner takes the
    mega-dojo's money and becomes its champion enforcer. You fight your way
    up through their organization, meeting your rival three times — each
    fight harder, each conversation shorter. **Hook:** the shonen rival arc
    distilled: the last battle isn't the boss, it's bringing your friend
    home for the true final fight.

12. **THE LAST DOJO** *(training arc)* — An unbeatable champion has closed
    every gym in the city by defeating its master — except one crumbling
    dojo run by a retired hermit who refuses students. Two stubborn trainees
    won't leave until he trains them. **Hook:** a pure training-arc first
    act (carry water, punch tires, run stairs) before the boss ladder even
    begins.

### C. Cyberpunk & high-concept skins

13. **SWEAT.EXE** — A viral AI fitness app hypnotizes the whole city into
    its Optimal Program — identical workouts, identical fighters, glowing
    headbands. Two offline trainees fight waves of brainwashed gym rats to
    unplug the server. **Hook:** beating a boss "frees" their discipline;
    the roster of the city's styles literally reboots as you win.

14. **THE GAINS THIEF** — A masked villain is draining fighters' strength
    overnight, leaving the city's strongest unable to lift an empty bar.
    Every boss you defeat gets their gains back, and you get stronger doing
    it. **Hook:** progression IS the plot — the level-up system and the
    story are the same sentence.

15. **NEO-MUSCLE CITY** — Cyberpunk gangs themed on fitness subcultures —
    the Crossfit Cult, the Powerlifting Syndicate, the Cardio Riders, the
    Yoga Assassins — carve up the city, each convinced their way is the only
    way. Two freelance trainees take them on district by district.
    **Hook:** every fallen gang leader opens their district's training style
    to everyone — unification as the win condition, subculture in-jokes as
    the fan service.

16. **PROTEIN WARS** — A cartel is spiking the city's supplement supply,
    turning honest lifters into rage-fueled muscle for hire. Fight from
    smoothie bars to shipping docks to the cartel's chrome tower. **Hook:**
    Streets of Rage's crime-syndicate plot in a gym skin — punchy, seedy,
    zero lore required.

### D. Wildcards

17. **ARCADE ETERNAL** *(retro meta)* — Two trainees are sucked into a dying
    90s arcade cabinet and must fight through its floors — each a loving
    pastiche of a different retro genre and art style — to earn their way
    out. **Hook:** deep-cut references as level design; the fan-service-
    dense structure Shredder's Revenge proved works.

18. **GHOST GYM** — An abandoned gym is haunted by the spirits of eight
    legendary masters, each guarding the secret of their discipline.
    Survive a night of spectral boss fights to inherit them all. **Hook:**
    one building, eight floors, eight styles — a vertical arcade gauntlet
    with a spooky palette shift per floor.

19. **TWO HALVES** — A legendary style was split for safekeeping: the male
    trainee inherited its strength half, the female its speed half, and
    neither works alone. A syndicate hunting the complete style forces them
    across the city to reunite. **Hook:** mechanics-as-story — co-op team-up
    moves literally reassemble the lost art, whose reunited name is
    **Training Mode**.

20. **MEMBERSHIP FEE** — The city's gym chain has a contract no one reads:
    the owner is a demon who collects years of your life as dues, and
    cancellation is only accepted in person. Two members decide to cancel.
    **Hook:** the universally relatable "gym membership you can't cancel"
    joke escalated into a boss-rush against middle-management demons.

## Part 3 — Merge Notes (designer suggestions)

- **Strongest seed pairing:** #1 Hostile Takeover × #2 Franchise Wars —
  the straight redemption arc in the cyberpunk skin is the closest match to
  the brand and the boss-caricature roster from `00-MASTER-CONCEPT.md` §7.
- **#5 Two Enforcers** solves the male/female dual-protagonist question
  structurally (each has their own half of the campaign, co-op as a story
  payoff) and can be layered onto any seed variation.
- **#19 Two Halves** gives the *name* "Training Mode" an in-world origin —
  worth stealing as an ending beat for whichever concept wins.
- **#10 100 Days** and **#12 The Last Dojo** are the most marketable to the
  gym×anime demographic (attemptable-challenge hooks) — either works as a
  prologue chapter or a DLC arc rather than the main plot.
- **#20 Membership Fee** is the most tonally Scott Pilgrim — a strong comedy
  B-plot or bonus stage even if not the main story.
